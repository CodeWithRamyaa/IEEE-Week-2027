# LITERATURE REVIEW

## Introduction

This section reviews existing research studies
related to social media usage among college students.

The purpose is to understand previous research
findings, research methods, and limitations.

## 1. Study by Wondwesen Tafesse (2022)

**Title:** Social Networking Sites Use and College Students' Academic Performance: Testing for an Inverted U-Shaped Relationship Using Automated Mobile App Usage Data.

**Objective:**

The study aimed to examine the relationship between social networking site usage and academic performance among college students.

**Methodology:**

The researcher collected social networking site usage data using a mobile application tracking method for one week. Academic performance information was obtained from college records.

**Major Findings:**

The study identified an inverted U-shaped relationship between social networking site usage and academic performance. Moderate usage was positively associated with academic performance, whereas heavier usage was negatively associated with it.

**Conclusion:**

The research highlights the importance of understanding social media usage intensity rather than assuming that all social media usage has the same academic impact.
## 2. Study by Xin Su and Jiatao Huang (2021)

**Title:** Social Media Use and College Students' Academic Performance: Student Engagement as a Mediator.

**Objective:**

The study aimed to investigate the relationship between social media usage, student engagement, and academic performance among college students.

**Methodology:**

The researchers collected survey responses from 307 Chinese college students and used statistical analysis to examine the relationships between the variables.

**Major Findings:**

The study found that student engagement played a mediating role in the relationship between social media usage and academic performance. However, the direct effect of social media usage on academic performance was not statistically significant.

**Conclusion:**

The research highlighted the importance of student engagement when understanding the relationship between social media usage and academic performance.
## 3. Study by Rana Al Mosharrafa, Taslima Akther and Fahimul Kader Siddique (2024)

**Title:** Impact of Social Media Usage on Academic Performance of University Students: Mediating Role of Mental Health Under a Cross-Sectional Study in Bangladesh.

**Objective:**

The study aimed to examine the relationship between social media usage, mental health and academic performance among university students.

**Methodology:**

The researchers collected data through online and offline surveys. Initially, 417 responses were collected, out of which 380 were selected for analysis. Statistical techniques and Structural Equation Modelling were used to examine the relationships between the variables.

**Major Findings:**

The study identified a significant relationship between social media usage, mental health and academic performance. Mental health played a mediating role in the relationship examined by the researchers.

**Conclusion:**

The research highlighted that social media usage, mental health and academic performance are interconnected and should be studied together to understand students' experiences.
# Research Gap

The reviewed studies have examined different aspects of social media usage among college students, including academic performance, student engagement and mental health.

However, further investigation can be conducted to understand how social media usage patterns differ among students across academic years and how educational and entertainment-related usage are associated with their academic habits.

The present study proposes to examine these aspects among its selected college-student population using survey-based data collection and statistical analysis.

Additionally, an interactive data visualization dashboard will be developed to present the collected data, identify usage patterns and communicate the research findings effectively.

The study will contribute a population-specific analysis of social media usage patterns and their relationship with academic activities.
